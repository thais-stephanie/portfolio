Five systems, one traceable number

Performance and compensation for the Success team were worked out by hand, in spreadsheets, from data that lived in five different places. Each source held one fragment. None held the relationship between them, and no rule said which one wins when they disagree.

01 · Why the number could not be trusted

Five unanswerable questions sat underneath every compensation cycle: which contract was in force, how much to attribute to each account, which manager receives that attribution, when an addition or cancellation counts, and which amount belongs in recurring revenue, churn or expansion. Everything else was investigation and interpretation, which is another way of saying the number depended on who worked it out.

02 · The monthly record

Each month's closing is stored as a validated state of the book. The following month opens from that close rather than being rebuilt from scratch. Month end stops being an excavation, because you explain the difference between two known states instead of reconstructing the state itself. And every figure gains somewhere to be traced back to: a movement points at an event, an event points at a ticket, a ticket points at a contract and a subscription.

03 · History had to exist first

A churn number asks about arrangements that ended, so a reconstruction that only sees what is currently active makes every historical movement invisible by construction. The contract count running near three times active headcount is the point, not an anomaly.

04 · The dashboard

The first version was built to do four things at once: bring the data together, show which movements were being counted, make the calculation legible to the people it describes, and expose the records that are simply wrong. A second version followed, shaped by what the first month of real use taught me.

05 · Then I audited my own work

A model that pays people has to survive being attacked by the person who built it. So I went back looking for reasons not to trust it: layer by layer reconciliation with an evidence trail, continuity testing across month boundaries, and targeted corrections where the source data was wrong rather than the measure.
