eight years, in detail.

Across process consulting, enterprise integrations, finance operations, product, revenue systems and AI, the work has been the same thing at growing scale:

understand a messy business process, find which facts and rules matter, design the system around them, connect the platforms it needs, and make the result reliable enough for people to depend on.

what I chose not to use.

Part of the job is knowing what doesn't belong in the architecture, and saying so.

AI for eligibility and scoring. In Career Agent, deterministic code owns the score and the hard gates. The model only interprets ambiguous text.
RAG where a query would do. Applied in document assistants, deliberately avoided wherever a deterministic lookup answers the question.
Browser automation for collection. Explored with Playwright, then deprioritised in favour of stable ATS APIs.
A duplicate-prevention system nobody asked for. Nearly finished for the requisition builder, then taken apart: duplicates were a side effect, not a business rule.
Corrupting a shared record to dodge a platform limit. Fountain locations stayed clean. I modeled around the constraint instead.
A CDP product. I haven't run one. I have solved the problem it solves, at Globalfy, by choosing the identity key first.

positions I can defend.

Opinions formed in production, the kind I'd hold in an architecture review.

  1. 1Integrations that move money are transactional systems, not automations.
  2. 2A system that runs without errors is not the same as a system that is correct.
  3. 3Prefer a rule you can test over a model you have to trust.
  4. 4Absence of evidence is not evidence. Unresolved is a valid answer.
  5. 5Evaluations only matter if they're allowed to say no.
  6. 6“The tool can't do that” usually means the interface can't. The API generally can.
  7. 7Adoption comes from what you cut before writing code. Automating a bad form makes it bad faster.
  8. 8The best automation isn't the one with no humans in it. It's the one where human attention only reaches the cases that need judgment.
