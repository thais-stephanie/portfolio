Local-first AI platform · public beta Python 3.12 · SQLite · JavaScript · Playwright

Which openings make sense for this person, and why?

A local-first job intelligence application that goes further than keyword matching. It ingests postings from 27 sources, lets a model interpret the unstructured text, and keeps eligibility, lifecycle and scoring in deterministic code, so every recommendation traces back to the evidence that produced it.

01 · Deterministic where it has to be

The model is good at reading loosely written text: a job description that buries the location rule in paragraph six, a résumé that describes the same skill three different ways. Eligibility, lifecycle and scoring must stay predictable, so the architecture keeps them in deterministic code.

Ingestion→Normalization→Structured extraction→Eligibility reasoning→Search Fit→Quote-verified deep reading→Explained recommendation
Eligibility
Gated before anything is scored
Hiring restrictions are kept separate from preferences. "Remote" does not mean worldwide, and a missing fact stays unresolved instead of being guessed.
Search Fit
Plain arithmetic over known facts
A transparent score over configured preferences and observed posting facts, with the reasons and the missing information visible. The job title does not buy fit points.
Quotes
Verifiable by construction
Text quoted from a posting remains an exact substring of the source, so every explanation can be checked against the original.
Find jobs. Jobs the person cannot apply to are hidden before anything is ranked, and each card says why it fits and what still has no proof. Synthetic demo data.
02 · Evidence the candidate confirms

The candidate profile is an evidence base that grows as the candidate reviews it. A claim extracted from a document is a suggestion until the candidate reviews it.

Review
Extracted claims can be confirmed, edited, rejected or superseded. Importing text does not confirm experience.
Generation
A sentence that a rule or an AI writes never becomes confirmed evidence.
Export
A resume line that cites evidence the person has not confirmed cannot be exported. Analyze names what it found and gives no score, and Resumes shows no match score at all.
Resume check against one job. Each thing the posting asks for is backed by proof, on the resume with no proof, or not found. Counts you can check, not a score. Synthetic demo data.

The experiment missed a threshold that was defined before it ran. The threshold did not move to meet the result, and production kept the existing deterministic score. It was the best candidate of a research program on semantic matching, and every candidate failed a gate fixed before its run. I closed the program and kept the last holdout sealed and unspent.

04 · Measuring the order

I measured the list against my own review of 181 postings the product had graded GOOD or STRONG. I kept 17 and rejected 164. The release that followed ordered the list by the deep reading and set 4,077 postings with no readable description aside as leads instead of giving them low scores. The first full deep reading round read 1,500 postings in 6.1 hours, and quote verification rejected none of them.

Every relevance label here is mine, so this measures the system against one person's judgment. The top 20 review still failed one of its own criteria, and recall across the whole market has never been measured.

05 · Local-first by design
Storage
Settings, jobs, notes and evidence live on the user's machine. Personal and demo data sit in separate directories.
Network
Collection is a deliberate user action. Optional AI providers receive content only when the user configures and uses them.
Privacy inventory
Every byte that leaves the computer is listed. Collecting jobs sends search phrases and places to job sites, never the CV. The local model refuses an address that is not local.
The Resumes workspace. Lines that no proof backs up are marked in yellow on the preview, and the resume exports to PDF, Word or JSON. Synthetic demo data.

07 · Sources and recovery

27 integrated sources cover employer hiring systems such as Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday, remote job boards, aggregators, and Brazilian and Latin American sites. I reviewed each source's terms of service and robots rules before building it, and did not build one remote hiring network whose terms forbid scraping. Ingestion paces requests per host, handles rate limits and backs off after transient failures. Each source's coverage is recorded, so a feed that exposes only metadata or a window of recent postings is never treated as a full inventory. A source that refuses or changes its format shows as failed, never as zero jobs.
