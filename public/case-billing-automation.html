Case study 01
In production since Jun 2026 HubSpot · Stripe · n8n · Airtable

Subscription billing, rebuilt so Finance could audit it instead of operating it

A two person Finance function carried payroll, refunds, reimbursements, credits, compliance and every change to client subscription billing. The billing queue alone ran at 60 to 80 tickets a week. Auditing was the first thing to fall off, which meant the detector for a billing error was the client noticing it.

01 · The problem

Every new client, every added role, every pay rate increase, every reduction from 40 hours to 30, every replacement when someone leaves, every cancellation: each one arrives as a HubSpot ticket and has to become the right change in Stripe, at the right price, with the right coupon, on the right billing anchor, with the right proration or credit attached.

One person cannot carry that queue and also check it. The same person was the throughput gate and the quality safety net, so any hour spent on one was taken from the other. In May 2026 both roles failed in the same week: a ticket said full time while the signed agreement said a 30 hour role, and the gap was only caught after the subscription had already been created.

03 · Architecture

Two n8n workflows, 250 nodes between them. The first one reads the request and decides what it is. The second one is the only thing allowed to touch Stripe, and it verifies its own work before closing the ticket.

04 · What it handles on its own
New client subscription Add product Price increase Price reduction Proration Discounts and card fees Credit balances Product removal Replacement swap Cancellation
Idempotency and locking
A retried ticket cannot double charge, and two requests on the same subscription cannot interleave.
Dead letter queue with aging
Failures land somewhere visible, get older in public, and are reconciled rather than silently retried forever.
Integer money arithmetic
No floats anywhere near a price, a proration or a credit. Business day and time zone handling on every anchor date.
Checksum gated deployment
Offline Node test harnesses replay real published node source against real payloads before anything ships.
05 · The audit layer that did not exist

Automating the queue was only half of it. The other half was building the comparison nobody had time to make: what the client signed, against what the client is being charged. Python parsed 1,168 signed agreements; the reconciliation layer compared them to live Stripe state and wrote every mismatch into a findings register that Finance works through.

07 · What I would flag to the next owner

The change types that launched behind an approval flag were behind it as a monitoring measure, not as permanent control. That period has since closed and the flag is gone: every request type, cancellations included, now applies automatically.

Upstream data entry is still the weak link. When the source record is wrong, the automation faithfully produces a wrong result. That belongs to the team that owns the record, and it is the next thing worth fixing.

The rules in the system are not mine. They were elicited from Finance, written down with confirmed against proposed tagging, and implemented only once confirmed. Where two statements contradicted each other, I pushed back rather than picking one.
