02 · Subscription billing automation · HubSpot ↔ Stripe ↔ n8n · live since Jun 2026

A two-person Finance team stopped operating billing and started auditing it.

I rebuilt the HubSpot-to-Stripe pipeline as two n8n workflows, 250 nodes, covering new clients, price changes, prorations, credits, replacements and cancellations. It stops and asks for a human only when something doesn't add up.

The goal was never to remove Finance from the loop. It was to change what they do in it. Auditing became a standing activity, month-end close improved, overtime came down, and the team didn't have to grow.

What I automate, and what I don't.

I automate the copying, re-typing, checking and routing. People keep the judgment calls, the client conversations, the exceptions and the problems that genuinely need a person.

And I didn't invent a single billing rule. I wrote them down with the people who own them, pushed back when two statements contradicted each other, and built whichever one was confirmed.

Finance went from operating billing to auditing it. The team didn't have to grow.
Success Managers got 20 to 51 hours back from requisitions alone.
Onboarding doubled weekly calls with the same people, by redistributing work.
  1. 1Facts before reasoning. Metrics are computed in code. The model reasons over results it can't change.
  2. 2Read-only first. Side effects need explicit permission, and a human when the risk is high.
  3. 3Assume it will fail. Retries that can't double charge. Queues that fail where people can see.
  4. 4Fail closed. A missing source stops publication. Nothing ships on a best guess.
