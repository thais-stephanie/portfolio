The meeting caps a CRM cannot enforce

Success Managers had started blocking their own calendars by hand. That is what a team does when the system assigning its work cannot be trusted. The cap that protected preparation time, one onboarding call a day and three a week, was tracked in a spreadsheet, and a spreadsheet does not push back.

01 · It was never a capacity problem

Six people at three calls a week is eighteen calls of headroom. Four or five were happening. The complaint arriving from the team was that this is too much, and the honest translation was that this is in the wrong places.

Had I read it as capacity, I would have argued for more Success Managers, or for relaxing the cap, or for shorter calls. All three would have been wrong. The work needed redistributing, not reducing. Native round robin distributes on calendar availability, which is a different question from who is eligible for the next call.

03 · Two constraints that shaped everything
The closing rep has to be in the meeting
Present in the invite from the moment the client books, not added afterwards. That ruled out anything that assembled the invite later.
The client picks the time
Whatever I built had to end in self service booking, so the assignment decision had to happen before the client ever saw a slot.
And one deliberate omission
The form does not show who was assigned or why. Information design was a control here: an assignment you can predict is an assignment you can game.
